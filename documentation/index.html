GADAPAD Documentation

A launchpad that only trusts what can be verified.

GADAPAD is a fair launch platform built natively on GIWA. Participants and project teams both get verified before anything happens, every launch runs on the same terms for everyone involved, and the funds raised stay under the control of the people who put them in.

Verified by Dojang Governed by participants Built on GIWA

The problem with launching in Korea today

Korea isn't short on builders, capital, or appetite for Web3. What it's missing is a launchpad people can actually trust. Every option out there, in Korea or anywhere else, tends to share the same two problems. There's rarely a real way to check who's actually behind a listing before any money moves, and once a raise wraps up, the people who funded it have no say left in what happens next. Trust only flows one way, from the participant to the team, and it stays that way.

GADAPAD flips that around. Participants shouldn't have to trust a team blindly, and a team shouldn't be able to walk off with a treasury the moment a raise hits its cap.

Three pillars

Verified participation

Every wallet that contributes, and every team that lists a project, holds a Dojang verified address attestation. That swaps pure anonymity for real accountability, without putting anyone's personal data on chain.

Curated listings

Every listing gets a minimum check from a recognized curator before it goes public. That's not a guarantee of quality, but it filters out projects that never should have gotten near a raise in the first place.

Participant governed treasury

Money raised in a launch lands in a dedicated treasury instead of a team's wallet. Getting it released takes approval from the people who put it there, tranche by tranche.

Who GADAPAD serves

Participants get a way to back new projects on GIWA with a verified counterparty and an actual say in how their money gets spent afterward. For project teams, it means access to an audience that already trusts the process, so they're not starting from zero on credibility. And curators get a role with real boundaries. They're strengthening the system, not vouching for any single outcome.

GADAPAD is infrastructure for other teams to launch fairly. It doesn't issue a token of its own. More on that in the FAQ.

For a launchpad, fast and predictable blocks actually matter. Why the name actually matters

기와, giwa, means roof tile. One tile by itself doesn't do much. Lay a whole course of them in overlapping rows, though, and they keep an entire building dry.

GIWA takes its name from the traditional Korean roof tile, small, identical pieces that only become a roof once they're laid together in overlapping rows. GIWA describes itself as an open layer 2 that belongs to no single entity, accessible to anyone building on it. That idea carries straight over into how GADAPAD treats a raise. No single big contribution should get to define a launch, and no single party should get to decide what happens to a treasury once people have put money into it. A launch holds together the way a roof does, through a lot of small, verified pieces stacked on top of each other under the same terms.

Dojang couldn't be rebuilt anywhere else

GADAPAD's verification, curation, and treasury design all lean on Dojang, the identity attestation service that lives on GIWA. Dojang isn't some generic KYC widget you could bolt onto any chain. It's issued through a regulated partner, anchored to the Ethereum Attestation Service, and wired straight into GIWA's own predeployed contracts. Build GADAPAD anywhere else, and you'd have to build an entire identity layer from scratch before the launchpad itself could even start. Build it on GIWA, and that layer's already there. How GADAPAD works

Every launch on GADAPAD moves through the same five stages in the same order, whether it's the first listing or the hundredth.

  1. 1ApplyCurator review
  2. 2VerifyOnce per wallet
  3. 3ContributeUniform terms
  4. 4ClaimYour allocation
  5. 5VoteRelease of funds

Stages 1 and 2 are gates, nothing opens until both clear. Stages 4 and 5 run on separate timelines, so getting your tokens never depends on how a treasury vote goes.

  1. Apply and get curated

    A project team submits an application tied to its own Dojang verified address, and a curator checks the team, the contract, and the proposed terms against a fixed bar before anything opens to the public.

  2. Verify

    Anyone who wants to contribute connects a wallet holding a Dojang Verified Address attestation. Verification happens once, through GIWA Wallet, and carries across every launch on GADAPAD after that.

  3. Contribute

    The contribution window opens for a fixed period. Every participant enters under the same rate, the same minimum, and the same maximum. The launch contract enforces the hardcap automatically and rejects anything that would exceed it.

  4. Claim

    Once the team activates claiming, participants withdraw the project tokens their contribution earned, according to whatever vesting the launch defined upfront.

  5. Vote to release

    The funds raised don't move to the team automatically. The team has to submit a treasury release request, and the participants who funded the raise vote on it before anything leaves the contract.

Why this order

Curation comes first because a listing needs to clear a bar before it's worth anyone's time verifying against it. Verification comes before contribution because checking identity after the money's already moved would defeat the whole point. And treasury release happens on its own, separate from claiming, since getting your tokens and deciding how a team spends what it raised are genuinely two different decisions. They deserve two different moments, not one.

Core Concepts

Dojang verification

Dojang is GIWA's identity attestation service, and it's the one piece of infrastructure GADAPAD is really built around.

What Dojang does

Dojang issues on chain attestations for off chain information, built on top of the Ethereum Attestation Service. Basically, it lets a wallet address prove something true about whoever's holding it, like having completed identity verification with a regulated issuer, without putting that person's actual name, birth date, or documents on chain anywhere. The chain only ever sees a reference to an attestation. The actual data stays with the issuer.

GIWA's own documentation calls Dojang the trust layer of its ecosystem, and that description actually holds up once you look at the mechanics. A smart contract can't check a passport. What it can do is check a boolean that some other contract already figured out for it.

How GADAPAD reads it

Every function on GADAPAD that moves money, whether that's a participant contributing to a launch or a team submitting one, sits behind a single check against Dojang's on chain query contract, DojangScroll.

Off chain
Identity check with the issuer
Name, documents, and the verification itself stay with Upbit Korea. None of it is written to GIWA.
On chain
DojangScroll.isVerified()
The launch contract asks one question and gets back one boolean. It never sees, or needs, the underlying identity.
Result
The transaction itself
Verified, proceeds
Not verified, reverts

The check runs at call time, every time. There is no cached status to go stale and no path through the contracts that skips this gate, for participants or for listing teams.

Solidity
interface IDojangScroll {
    function isVerified(address addr, bytes32 attesterId)
        external view returns (bool);
}

abstract contract VerifiedAddressAccessControl {
    address internal constant DOJANG_SCROLL =
        0xd5077b67dcb56caC8b270C7788FC3E6ee03F17B9;

    error NotVerified();

    modifier onlyVerified(bytes32 attesterId) {
        if (!IDojangScroll(DOJANG_SCROLL).isVerified(msg.sender, attesterId)) {
            revert NotVerified();
        }
        _;
    }
}

If that check fails, the transaction reverts before any money moves. There's no path through GADAPAD's contracts that skips this gate, not for participants and not for the teams listing a project.

What gets verified today

Dojang issues several kinds of attestation. GADAPAD only relies on one of them right now.

AttestationWhat it confirmsUsed by GADAPAD
Verified AddressThe wallet has completed identity verification with a recognized issuerRequired
Balance RootA signed snapshot of balances at a point in timeNot used
Verified BalanceA specific account balance at a specific timestampNot used
Verified CodeAn off chain authentication code, confirmed on chainNot used

Right now, Upbit Korea is the only issuer of Verified Address attestations on GIWA. Dojang's design leaves room for more issuers down the line, and GADAPAD will accept whichever ones GIWA recognizes without needing a contract change, since the attester gets passed in as a parameter instead of hardcoded into the launch logic.

What this check actually proves

Let's be precise about what this actually guarantees. Dojang verification confirms a wallet address is linked to a completed identity check at a regulated issuer. It doesn't guarantee that one human only controls one verified wallet, and honestly, no launchpad can claim that. What it does do is make abuse expensive. Opening a new verified identity takes real effort in the real world. Spinning up a fresh wallet costs nothing. That gap, between free and costly, is what actually makes fair distribution enforceable.

Curated listings

Verification answers who someone is. Curation answers whether their project belongs on GADAPAD in the first place. They're independent checks, and GADAPAD requires both.

Identity
Verification
Answers who is behind a wallet. Runs automatically, on chain, for every participant and every listing team.
+
Eligibility
Curation
Answers whether a project belongs here at all. Runs once per listing, by a named person against a fixed checklist.

Two independent questions, so passing one says nothing about the other. A verified team can still be rejected in curation, and curation never substitutes for the on chain check.

Why permissionless listing isn't enough on its own

A launchpad that lets anyone list anything hasn't gotten rid of risk. It's just moved it, from the launchpad's front door onto every individual participant, who now has to do their own diligence on every single listing with zero support. GADAPAD adds a layer in front of that decision. By the time a project reaches a public contribution window, a curator has already looked at it.

What curation checks

  • The team behind the project holds its own Dojang verified address, and that identity is on record with GADAPAD.
  • The launch contract matches GADAPAD's audited template, with no unexpected admin function that could move participant funds outside the treasury flow.
  • The project hasn't already raised from investors ahead of its GADAPAD listing, in line with the fair launch standard.
  • The project is deployed, or committed to deploying, on GIWA itself.
  • Public claims about the project hold up, nothing obviously false or misleading.

What curation doesn't cover

Curation means a project cleared a fixed checklist. That's it. It's not a financial audit, investment advice, or a promise that the project succeeds or its token holds value. You're still responsible for your own decisions here. More in Risk Disclosures.

Who curators are

Curators are recognized members of the GIWA ecosystem who are individually accountable, verified through Dojang just like everyone else on the platform. Their identity is attached to every review they complete, so this isn't some anonymous rubber stamp. The full mandate and checklist live on the Curators page.

The fair launch standard

Every project on GADAPAD launches under the same standard. No exceptions, no side deals worked out off the record.

No prior investors

If a project already sold a private allocation to investors before showing up on GADAPAD, it's not eligible to list. Curators catch this during review, so it's not something a participant has to discover the hard way later. The reasoning here is simple. A wallet sitting on a large, already unlocked position from a private round has both the means and the motive to dump on the first public buyers. GADAPAD's public window is supposed to be the first chance to buy in, not the last stop before insiders cash out.

Uniform terms for every participant

One rate
The same conversion rate for the whole window. Arriving early buys nothing extra.
A floor and a ceiling
Every verified address is bound by the same minimum and maximum, so no wallet can dominate a launch.
A hardcap
The contract rejects any contribution that would push the total past the cap set at configuration.

All three are contract parameters, fixed before a listing opens. They are checked in curation and then enforced on chain, not promised in a blog post.

  • One rate. Every contribution during the window converts to project tokens at the same fixed rate, regardless of when in the window it lands or how large it is.
  • A minimum and a maximum. Each launch sets a floor and a ceiling per verified address, so a launch can't get dominated by a handful of large wallets.
  • A hardcap. Total contributions can't exceed the cap the project set during configuration. The contract rejects any contribution that would push the total past it.

Nothing large to dump on day one

Since prior investor allocations are excluded entirely, and any allocation the team keeps for itself streams or vests over time instead of unlocking all at once, there's no big, freely tradable position just sitting there the moment claiming opens. The people in the best position to sell right away are the same participants who bought in under the same public terms as everyone else.

On GADAPAD, fair launch isn't a marketing line. It's a set of contract parameters that get checked before a listing opens and verified on chain once it does.

Core Concepts

Participant governed treasury

This is the piece that actually separates GADAPAD from a normal launchpad. When a launch window closes, the money raised doesn't just become the team's. It goes into a treasury that the people who funded it still have a say over.

What GADAPAD refuses to do

On most launchpads, once a raise clears, the money moves straight to a wallet the project team fully controls. From there, the participants who funded it have no real way to act. They can watch. They can call the team out publicly if things go sideways. But the contract itself gives them nothing to actually do. GADAPAD treats that gap as the real failure point here, not something to just shrug off as normal.

How release works

Funds contributed to a launch settle into a treasury contract scoped to that launch alone. Reaching the hardcap does not unlock them. Instead, releasing funds to the project team follows a fixed sequence.

  1. 1RequestAmount and purpose
  2. 2ReviewFixed window
  3. 3VoteWeighted by contribution
  4. 4SettleRelease or hold

There is no path that skips a stage. A window closing without quorum resolves to hold, never to release, so silence can never move money.

  1. The team submits a release request

    A request states an amount and a stated purpose, tied to the team's verified address. Requests can cover the full treasury or a tranche of it.

  2. A fixed review window opens

    Every participant who contributed to that launch can review the request during a set period before any vote closes.

  3. Participants vote

    Voting weight is proportional to the amount a participant contributed. The people who put the most at risk carry the most say over what happens to it.

  4. The treasury settles the outcome

    If the request clears quorum and threshold, the requested amount releases to the team. If it doesn't, the funds stay put and the team can revise and resubmit.

Funds never default to release. If a window closes without quorum, the treasury just stays exactly where it was. Nothing moves without an actual on chain yes vote.

Why tranches instead of one lump sum

Asking for funds in tranches, each tied to a stated purpose, gives participants a repeated checkpoint instead of one decision made before a project has shipped a single thing. A team that delivers keeps earning approval. A team that goes quiet has to explain itself to the same people who funded the first tranche before it sees another one.

Getting verified

Verification happens once, outside GADAPAD entirely, and then carries across every launch on the platform afterward.

  1. Open GIWA Wallet

    Dojang verification happens through GIWA Wallet, not GADAPAD itself. If your wallet's already verified, you're done with this step.

  2. Complete identity verification with an issuer

    Right now that means verifying through Upbit Korea, the only issuer Dojang currently recognizes. Your identity info stays with the issuer and never touches GIWA directly.

  3. Receive a Verified Address attestation

    Once verification's done, an attestation referencing your wallet address gets issued on chain through the Ethereum Attestation Service. Only the reference is public. Your personal data isn't.

  4. Connect the same wallet to GADAPAD

    GADAPAD checks your connected address against DojangScroll before letting you contribute or apply to launch anything. A verified wallet passes right away, nothing else to do on GADAPAD's end.

One verification, every launch

The attestation lives on GIWA itself, not inside GADAPAD's contracts, so it's not tied to any one launch or even to GADAPAD specifically. Any Dojang integrated app recognizes the same attestation. Verify once, and that covers every launch after it.

Check verification status any time by calling isVerified(address, attesterId) on DojangScroll at 0xd5077b67dcb56caC8b270C7788FC3E6ee03F17B9 on GIWA Sepolia.

For Participants

Participating in a launch

Once a wallet is verified, joining a live launch is a short, bounded set of decisions, not some open ended negotiation.

  1. Review the launch terms

    Every listing publishes its rate, minimum contribution, maximum contribution per address, hardcap, and window before it opens. These terms are fixed once the window starts and cannot change mid launch.

  2. Connect a verified wallet

    Contribution is only available to addresses holding a current Verified Address attestation. An unverified wallet gets pointed to Getting Verified before it can proceed.

  3. Contribute within the window

    Contributions settle in the stablecoin configured for that launch. The contract checks the minimum, the per address maximum, and the remaining room under the hardcap before accepting any amount.

  4. Hold the allocation until claiming opens

    A contribution immediately determines a token allocation at the fixed rate. The tokens themselves become available once the project activates claiming, covered on the next page.

What participants contribute in

Contributions settle in whatever stablecoin a launch is set up to accept. On testnet right now, that's a settlement token standing in to model the whole contribution and allocation flow before a live deployment exists. Once a KRW pegged stablecoin actually becomes available on GIWA, GADAPAD is built to accept it directly as a launch's settlement currency, which would give that asset one of its first real, everyday uses on chain instead of a purely speculative one.

Where enforcement actually happens

The minimum, the maximum, and the hardcap aren't just policy text published next to a launch. They're conditions checked inside participate() on every single call. A contribution that would blow past the per address maximum or push the total over the hardcap reverts before it's ever accepted, so nobody's waiting around for a refund afterward.

For Participants

Claiming and treasury voting

Contributing to a launch is where a participant's involvement starts, not where it ends. Two more actions follow, on two different timelines.

Your contribution
One action during the launch window, which earns you two separate rights.
Your tokens
Claiming
Withdraw what your contribution already earned, on the schedule the launch set. Nobody votes on this.
The team's spending
Treasury voting
Approve or reject each release request, weighted by what you contributed. Runs for as long as the treasury holds funds.

Kept apart on purpose. Claiming settles what you are already owed, voting decides what happens next, and folding them together would let one hold the other hostage.

Claiming your allocation

Once the project team turns claiming on, every participant can withdraw the project tokens their contribution earned. If the launch set up a vesting or streaming schedule for participant allocations, claiming only pulls whatever portion has actually unlocked by that point, not the full amount right away.

Solidity
function claim() external onlyVerified(ATTESTER_ID) nonReentrant {
    require(isClaimActive, "Claiming not active");
    require(!hasClaimed[msg.sender], "Already claimed");
    uint256 allocation = contributed[msg.sender] / rate;
    hasClaimed[msg.sender] = true;
    require(projectToken.transfer(msg.sender, allocation), "Transfer failed");
    emit Claimed(msg.sender, allocation);
}

Voting on treasury release

Separately from claiming, any participant who contributed to a launch can vote on treasury release requests from that project's team. A dashboard tied to your connected wallet shows open requests across every launch you contributed to, along with the amount requested, the stated purpose, and how much time's left in the review window. Your vote is weighted by how much you originally contributed, and you can cast it once per request.

Why these stay separate

Claiming is about getting what your contribution already earned. Voting is about deciding what happens next with the money a team wants to spend. Mashing these into one action would blur two decisions that each deserve their own attention, so GADAPAD keeps them as two completely separate flows.

Application and curation process

Listing on GADAPAD begins with an application tied to a verified address and ends with a curator's signature before anything goes public.

  1. Submit an application

    The team's verified address, a description of the project, the contract to be reviewed, and proposed launch parameters, including rate, hardcap, and contribution limits, are submitted together.

  2. Curator review

    An assigned curator works through the checklist covered on the Curators page. Curators may request changes to a contract or to proposed parameters before approving a listing.

  3. Approval and scheduling

    Once approved, the team and curator agree on a launch date. GADAPAD lists the launch with its verified terms visible ahead of the contribution window opening.

  4. Launch goes live

    The contribution window opens exactly as scheduled, under the exact terms that were reviewed. Nothing about the terms changes between approval and launch day.

Curators aim to complete an initial review within two weeks of submission. That's a target, not a promise, and a project needing revisions will take longer.

Role, mandate, and review checklist

Curators are the one point in GADAPAD's design where a human judgment sits in front of a contract. The role is deliberately narrow.

The mandate

A curator confirms a project clears a fixed, published checklist. That's it. They're not running financial due diligence, not giving investment advice, and not personally vouching for how a project performs down the line. Every review gets attached to the curator's own Dojang verified identity, so the checklist works as a record, not some anonymous gate.

Review checklist

Identity confirmed

The team's address holds a current Dojang Verified Address attestation.

Contract reviewed

Deployed source matches GADAPAD's launch template, with no undisclosed admin path to participant funds.

Fair launch confirmed

No prior investor allocation, and proposed terms are uniform for every participant.

Claims reviewed

Public statements about the project are checked against what the contract and team can actually support.

GIWA deployment confirmed

The project is live, or has a credible plan to go live, on GIWA specifically.

Code of conduct

A curator can't hold a financial stake in a project they're reviewing, can't take compensation from a team outside GADAPAD's standard process, and has to disclose any existing relationship with a team before taking on a review. If a review turns out to have ignored the checklist, that curator can be removed from the pool.

Architecture overview

GADAPAD is intentionally thin. It doesn't reimplement identity verification, and it doesn't hold funds outside a launch's own treasury. It just coordinates pieces that already exist, each built for its own purpose.

Identity
GIWA Wallet
Where a user completes Dojang verification and signs every GADAPAD transaction.
Attestation
Dojang and EAS
Issues and stores the Verified Address attestation GADAPAD reads before every action.
Per launch
Launch Vault
Accepts contributions, enforces caps, tracks allocation, and handles claiming for a single project.
Post launch
Treasury Governor
Holds raised funds and releases them only on a participant approved request.
Everything settles on GIWA. No off chain database of contributions or votes sits beside this. Whatever the chain records is the entire record.

GADAPAD owns only the last two boxes. Identity and attestation are GIWA's own infrastructure, which is why the same launchpad could not simply be rebuilt on another chain.

One vault per launch

Every project gets its own Launch Vault deployment instead of sharing a pooled contract with every other listing on GADAPAD. A bug, a dispute, or some unusual configuration in one launch has no way to touch any other. Funds, allocation records, and claim state all stay scoped to the one project that raised them.

Treasury Governor sits downstream

When a Launch Vault's contribution window closes, the raised balance moves into that launch's Treasury Governor instead of going straight to the team. The Governor has no owner controlled way to withdraw anything. Funds only leave through a release request that clears a participant vote, exactly like the Participant Governed Treasury page describes.

Everything settles on GIWA

Every contract in this stack, from the wallet interaction all the way to the final treasury release, runs and settles on GIWA. GADAPAD doesn't keep some off chain database of contributions or votes on the side. Whatever the chain records is the entire record.

Security model and network information

GADAPAD's security posture rests on a handful of enforced properties, not a long list of assumptions.

Enforced by the contracts
  • No unilateral withdrawal. No address, including GADAPAD's own deployer, moves funds out of a Treasury Governor without a passed vote.
  • Verified only access. Every fund moving function reads a live Dojang attestation at call time.
  • Launch isolation. Each project's vault and governor are separate deployments.
  • Reentrancy guards. On contribution, claiming, and treasury release alike.
Not claimed
  • No third party audit yet. What is described here is the intended design as it stands on testnet.
  • One human, one wallet. Verification raises the cost of abuse, it does not make it impossible.
  • Project outcomes. Curation is a checklist, never a judgment that a project will succeed.
  • Mainnet readiness. Nothing here should be treated as a live financial product yet.

The right hand column matters as much as the left. A security page that only lists guarantees is telling you half the story.

Enforced properties

  • Reentrancy protection. Every function that moves funds carries a reentrancy guard, including contribution, claiming, and treasury release.
  • No unilateral withdrawal. No address, including GADAPAD's own deployer, can move funds out of a Treasury Governor without a passed participant vote.
  • Verified only access. Every fund moving function checks a live Dojang attestation at call time, not some cached or stale status.
  • Launch isolation. Each project's Launch Vault and Treasury Governor are independent deployments, so one launch's mess can't reach another.

Audit status

GADAPAD's contracts haven't gone through a formal third party audit yet. What's described here is the intended design as it currently stands on testnet. An independent audit is planned before any mainnet deployment, and this page gets updated with the result once that's done.

Responsible disclosure

GIWA runs a chain level security council and bug bounty program covering the network GADAPAD sits on. GADAPAD follows the same idea at the application layer. If you find a vulnerability in GADAPAD's contracts, report it privately before going public with it, so a fix can reach participants before an exploit does.

Glossary

Terms used throughout this documentation, defined the way GADAPAD uses them specifically.

Attestation
An on chain record, issued through the Ethereum Attestation Service, confirming that something is true about an address without exposing the underlying data.
Curator
A recognized, Dojang verified reviewer who checks a project against GADAPAD's published checklist before it can list.
Dojang
GIWA's identity attestation service. Issues Verified Address and related attestations that GADAPAD checks before any fund movement.
DojangScroll
The on chain contract GADAPAD calls to check whether an address holds a valid attestation from a given issuer.
EAS
Ethereum Attestation Service, the underlying protocol Dojang is built on for issuing and storing attestations.
Fair launch
A launch with no prior investor allocation and uniform rate, minimum, and maximum terms for every participant.
Hardcap
The maximum total contribution a launch will accept before the contract stops allowing further contributions.
Launch Vault
The contract deployed for a single project's launch. Handles contribution, allocation tracking, and claiming.
Settlement currency
The stablecoin a given launch is configured to accept contributions in.
Treasury Governor
The contract holding a launch's raised funds after its window closes. Releases funds only through a participant approved request.
Verified Address
The specific Dojang attestation confirming a wallet has completed identity verification with a recognized issuer. See Dojang Verification.

Risk disclosures

Read this page before contributing to, or launching, anything on GADAPAD.

Nothing here is financial, legal, or investment advice. GADAPAD and its curators don't recommend any project, and curation isn't a guarantee of a project's performance, security, or legitimacy.

Testnet status

GADAPAD currently runs on GIWA Sepolia, a public testnet. The contracts haven't gone through an independent audit. What's described in this documentation reflects the intended design and could change before mainnet.

Smart contract risk

Interacting with any smart contract, audited or not, carries some risk of unexpected behavior, up to and including losing everything you put in. Only contribute an amount you can afford to lose completely.

Curation isn't the same as diligence

A curator's review confirms a project cleared a defined, published checklist. It doesn't confirm the team, business, or token will succeed, and it's not a substitute for an actual financial audit of the project.

What identity verification doesn't guarantee

Dojang verification confirms a wallet address is linked to a completed identity check with a recognized issuer. It doesn't guarantee that one person only controls one verified address, and it doesn't evaluate the merits of whatever project a verified address decides to launch or fund.

Regulatory status

Digital asset regulation varies by jurisdiction and keeps changing. Participants and project teams are responsible for understanding and following the laws that apply to them. GADAPAD doesn't provide legal guidance for any jurisdiction.
